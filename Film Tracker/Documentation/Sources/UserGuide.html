Chapter 1

Getting started

Film Tracker keeps a record of the films you own or care about: what they are, when you watched them, which discs you have bought, and which you still want. It manages information about your films, not the video files themselves.

The window

  • The sidebar on the left chooses what the window shows: the whole library, a built-in view such as Recents or Reports, a smart list, or one of your own lists. Hide or show it with ⌥⌘S.
  • The gallery in the middle shows the films in that view, as a grid of posters or as a list. A title bar above it holds the view’s controls.
  • The contextual sidebar on the right shows the selected film in three tabs: Details, Releases, and Notes (Chapter 4).
  • The toolbar holds the Add Film, Import, New List, and Scan for Duplicates buttons and the search field.
  • The status bar along the bottom counts the films in the view and how many are selected. It can be turned off in Settings > General.

Five ideas the rest of the app builds on

IdeaWhat it means
FilmOne entry in your library: title, release date, people, genre, your rating, poster, and many optional details.
Viewing eventA dated record that you watched the film, optionally noting which version (Theatrical, Extended, and so on). Together they form the film’s Viewing History.
RotationHow long a film rests after you watch it before it is due again. When that time has passed, the film is up for rotation.
ReleaseA physical disc edition of a film (4K UHD, Blu-ray, or DVD) that you have purchased or want to.
ListA named group of films. You make your own; smart lists fill themselves; the Queue holds what you plan to watch next.

A first session

  1. Add some films. Click Add Film (⌥A) to enter one by hand, or choose File > Import Metadata… (⇧⌘I) to add many at once from MP4/M4V files (Chapter 9). A film needs only a name and a release date.
  2. Record a viewing. Select a film, then click Add Viewing Event in the contextual sidebar and pick the date you watched it.
  3. Rate it. Press ⌘E to edit the film and set its User Rating from 0 to 10, where 0 means not rated.
  4. Add a disc. Press ⌘R to open the film’s releases, click Add New Release, and fill in what you know.
  5. Turn on automatic backups. Settings > General > Automatic database backup keeps the three most recent backups for you (Chapter 10).
Tip

Almost every button has a menu equivalent and a keyboard shortcut. The menus are the quickest way to discover what is possible from wherever you are, and Chapter 12 lists every shortcut.

Chapter 2

Browsing the gallery

Every view in the sidebar fills the same gallery, and the same tools apply wherever you are: grid or list, sort, filter, select, act.

The views in the sidebar

RecentsRecent activity in five tabs (Chapter 8).⌘3
ReportsSpending, prices, rankings, wishlist, and statistics (Chapter 8).⌘4
ImportsImport tools and the history of past imports (Chapter 9).
QueueFilms you plan to watch, in Up Next, Soon, and Later (Chapter 5).⌘5

Views with tabs (Unwatched, Recents, Reports, Imports, the Queue, and each smart list family) show a picker at the top of the gallery. Switch tabs with ⌥⌘1 through ⌥⌘9, or step through them with ⌘↑ and ⌘↓.

Grid and list

Press ⌘K (View > View as List / View as Grid) to switch between poster grid and list. The list view shows columns of details and is easier to scan for numbers and dates. The Large poster images setting makes the grid use fewer, larger posters on big windows.

Sorting

Choose View > Sort By, or the sort button in the gallery’s title bar. You can sort by Title, Year, Duration, Date Added, Date Modified, Date Purchased, Last Viewed, Recorded View Count, or Rating, ascending or descending. Options that cannot mean anything in the current view are dimmed; for example, Last Viewed on the Unwatched tab.

  • Section dividers split the gallery into labeled bands that follow the sort, such as one band per year, per letter of the title, or per rating. Turn them on or off in the same menu.
  • Jump to Section, in the same menu, scrolls straight to a band.
  • Title sorting uses a film’s Sort Name when it has one (Metadata tab), so “The Godfather” can sort under G.

Filtering

View > Filter (or the filter button in the title bar) narrows the current view without searching. Filters come in pairs, and you can combine pairs:

FilterKeeps
Watched / UnwatchedFilms marked, or not marked, as watched.
Color / Black and WhiteFilms by their Color setting.
Purchased / UnpurchasedFilms with at least one purchased release, or with none purchased.
Complete Data / Incomplete DataFilms by their Complete Data flag. Shown only when that feature is enabled in Settings > Advanced.
GenresAction and Adventure, Comedy, Drama, Horror, Kids and Family, Sci-Fi and Fantasy, Thriller. Several genres together keep films in any of them.

Choose None to clear every filter. With Remember the last-selected view options turned on in Settings > General, each view keeps its own sort, filter, and grid or list style. Your own lists always remember theirs.

Selecting and acting on films

  • Click to select; ⌘-click and ⇧-click to select several. ⌘A selects everything in the view.
  • Press Tab to move keyboard focus into the gallery, then use the arrow keys.
  • Secondary-click a film (or use the Film menu) to add it to the Queue or a list, see which lists it is in, edit it, remove it from a list, or delete it.
  • ⌘L (View > Scroll to Selection) brings the selected film back into view after scrolling away.
  • Drag films onto a list or the Queue in the sidebar to add them there.

Searching

The search field in the toolbar (⌘F) searches the whole library from any view. Plain text matches titles. Typing # starts a token that asks about any other field, such as #director kubrick or #rating 8+. Clearing the search returns you to the view you came from. The Film Tracker Search Guide explains every token.

Removing versus deleting

Remove from List (⌥⌫) takes a film out of the list you are viewing. Remove from All Lists (⇧⌥⌫) takes it out of every list and the Queue. Delete from Library (⌘⌫) removes the film, with its viewings and releases, from Film Tracker entirely. By default you are asked to confirm deletions; Settings > General controls those warnings.

Chapter 3

Adding and editing films

Every film is created and changed in the film sheet. Open it with Add Film (⌥A) for a new film, or with Edit Metadata… (⌘E) or the Edit button in the contextual sidebar for the selected one.

The sheet has five tabs: Details, Metadata, Releases, Notes, Poster. Switch with ⌥1 through ⌥5, or step with ⌘↑ and ⌘↓. Nothing is saved until you click Add or Update; Cancel discards every change made in the sheet. Only Name and Release Date are required.

Details

FieldPurpose
NameThe title shown everywhere. Required.
Release DateRequired. Its year is the film’s year, and viewing dates cannot be earlier than it (see Settings > Advanced to override that once).
User RatingYour rating, 0 to 10. 0 means Not Rated.
Genre / RatingOne of the seven genres, and the MPAA content rating.
Queue MembershipWhich Queue section the film is in, if any. The option beneath it adds the film to a chosen section automatically when it comes up for rotation (Chapter 5).
Duration / EditedThe runtime. For an edited film, also its edited runtime, which then counts as its length everywhere.
Viewing EventsThe film’s viewing history. Add (⌥A), change, or remove dated viewings and their versions here.
List MembershipThe lists the film belongs to.
SynopsisA plot summary. Searchable with the Synopsis token.
WatchedMarks the film as watched. Adding a viewing event sets this for you; check it by hand for films you saw before you started keeping dates.
EditedMarks a film you have re-edited yourself. Edited films appear in the Edited smart list and can carry edit notes (Notes tab).
Hall of FameAdds the film to your Hall of Fame ranking (Chapter 8). At most 200 films.
Complete DataA flag you set when a film’s information is finished. Shown only when enabled in Settings > Advanced.
Special SortingStatus tags that keep a film out of rotation and out of the Filtered Library (Chapter 7).
Rotation ThresholdUse Default, or a period of its own for this film (Chapter 5).
External IDsThe film’s IMDb.com and Blu-ray.com numbers, so the Web Query button can open its pages directly.

Metadata

The descriptive and technical record of the film, mostly filled in by imports (with a single film selected, ⌥⌘E opens the sheet directly on this tab):

  • People and companies: Cast, Director, Producer, Executive Producer, Screenwriter, Composer, Studio. Each holds several names; add or remove rows as needed.
  • Titles: Sort Name (used for title sorting) and any Alternate Titles (matched by searches and by ratings imports).
  • Your copy: Digital Source (the disc or download tier it came from, optionally remastered), Version and Subtitles, Included Versions, Aspect Ratio and Frame Rate, Color (including Colorized and Black-and-White), Encoding Tools, and HandBrake RF Value.
  • Text: Notes, iTunes Comments, and Tags. Tags are free-form labels that you can search for with the Tags token.

Releases

The film’s disc editions. This tab has enough to it that Chapter 6 covers it on its own. ⌘R opens the sheet directly on this tab.

Notes

A worksheet for films you edit yourself. It records the release your edit was made from, the software tools and HandBrake RF value used, the edit date, any warnings, a description of the process, notes on audio timing and special audio, and general notes.

The HandBrake Frames table lists the cut points of the edit as frame numbers with matching time codes. Each entry can be marked with an asterisk, a caret, or a number to point to a note, and can note a Final Cut Pro audio fade (Linear, S-curve, +3dB, or −3dB). Bulk Import fills both columns at once from comma-separated lists, which is quickest when copying from another tool.

Poster

Click Select An Image… to add or replace the poster, or Delete Poster to remove it (Undo Delete restores it until you save). Imports add posters automatically from the file’s artwork.

Note

The Details tab’s Release Date and the Metadata tab’s Sort Name are the two fields that most change how a film sorts. If a film appears in the wrong place, check those first.

Chapter 4

The contextual sidebar

Everything about the selected film, without opening the sheet. Switch tabs with ⌥1 (Details), ⌥2 (Releases), and ⌥3 (Notes).

The header

The poster, your rating, the year, content rating, and genre, with an Edit button that opens the film sheet.

Details tab

  • Add Viewing Event records a viewing (Chapter 5).
  • Web Query, the globe button beside it, opens the film on IMDb.com or Blu-ray.com in your browser, or searches for it there if its External IDs are not set. It can be hidden in Settings > General.
  • Watch details: Last Watched, Next Up for Rotation, Recorded View Count, Average Between Viewings, Duration, and Source for Viewing: what you would watch the film from (a disc you own, or its digital source), with a note when a disc in a format you do not own is available to buy.
  • Viewing History lists every viewing with the time since the one before it. Additional columns, such as the version watched, can be turned on in Settings > General.
  • List Membership shows the lists, smart lists, and Queue section the film is in. Click a list to open it with the film selected (the same as Film > Show in List).
  • Synopsis and Metadata show the rest of the film’s record. In the Cast and Crewmen section, the magnifying glass beside each name searches the library for that person or studio.

Releases tab

A summary of each release (format, studio, purchase status, release date, price or cost, region, HDR, audio, artwork) and, if you turn it on in Settings, a compact table summarizing the key details of each release, so you can glance over all of a film’s releases in one small place instead of reading every card.

A release that has not come out yet is marked as such: its year badge is a dashed outline instead of a filled pill, its date reads Releases on rather than Released on, and its year is grayed in the summary table. All three change on their own on the release day.

Notes tab

The film sheet’s Notes tab, read-only: the edit record and the HandBrake Frames table with its legend.

Tip

Settings > General > Contextual sidebar decides whether the sidebar stays on the same tab when you select another film, and whether collapsed tables open automatically.

Chapter 5

Watching and rotation

Record each viewing as it happens, and Film Tracker works out what is due to be watched again and keeps your plans in the Queue.

Recording a viewing

  1. Select the film and click Add Viewing Event in the contextual sidebar. (In the film sheet’s Details tab, press ⌥A instead.)
  2. Pick the date. By default it cannot be earlier than the film’s release date.
  3. Optionally pick the Version you watched: Theatrical, Extended, Director’s Cut, Special Edition, Alternate Ending, Unrated, Television Cut, Workprint, International Cut, “The Final Cut”, Custom Edit, Colorized, Black-and-white, or Other.
  4. Click Add. The film is marked as watched, its Last Watched date moves forward if this viewing is the newest, and its rotation date is recalculated.

A film can have one viewing per day. To change or remove a viewing, edit the film and use the Viewing Events table in the Details tab.

Adding the viewing to your calendar

With System calendar integration turned on in Settings > General, the same popover can also put the viewing in your Mac’s calendar. Check Add Event to System Calendar, choose a start time and a calendar, and the event is created with the film’s runtime as its length (the popover shows when it will end). macOS asks once for permission to use your calendars. Film Tracker only reads the list of calendars and writes the events you add.

Rotation

A film’s rotation threshold is how long it rests after your most recent viewing before it is up for rotation (due to be watched again). The choices are 1, 1½, 2, 3, 4, and 5 years.

  • The default threshold for every film is set in Settings > General.
  • An override gives one film its own threshold: edit the film and change Rotation Threshold from Use Default. Reset All Overrides in Settings returns every film to the default.
  • Due dates round up to the first of the following month. A film last watched on March 14, 2024 with a 1-year threshold comes up for rotation on April 1, 2025.

The contextual sidebar’s Next Up for Rotation field shows the due date and the time left, or Ready for viewing once it has arrived, or the Queue section the film is waiting in. Two smart lists collect these films: Up for Rotation (due under its threshold) and Viewed Over One Year Ago.

What rotation ignores

Films with no viewing date are never up for rotation, since there is nothing to count from. Films carrying any Special Sorting tag (Chapter 7) are left out of both rotation lists, whatever their dates.

The Queue

The Queue (⌘5) is your viewing plan, in three sections: Up Next, Soon, and Later. A film is in at most one section. The Queue’s All tab shows the three sections together as bands; the other tabs show one section each.

  • Adding: Film > Add to Queue, the secondary-click menu, the film sheet’s Queue Membership picker, or drag films onto Queue in the sidebar. Dragged films go to the section chosen in Settings > General.
  • Moving: choose a different section from the same menus (which read Manage Queue Membership once a film is queued). The film leaves its old section.
  • Removing: in the Queue, press ⌥⌫ (Remove from List).
  • Automatically: in the film sheet, check Add to … Queue when up for rotation and choose a section. The film is queued for you when it comes due.

Settings > General can keep queued films out of the Up for Rotation lists (so those lists show only what you have not yet planned), and can warn before you queue a film that carries a Special Sorting tag.

The Unwatched view

The Unwatched view (⌘2) has two tabs: Unwatched, films not marked as watched, and Not Rated, films whose rating is 0.

Chapter 6

Releases

A release is one disc edition of a film. Record the ones you own to track what you paid, and the ones you want to track prices and build a wishlist.

Working with releases

Press ⌘R (Film > Edit Releases…) to open the film sheet on its Releases tab. Each release is a card in a carousel; the arrows and the menu at the top move between them.

A film can hold up to ten cards. A combo pack counts as one card, including its bundled discs.

Combo packs

Set a 4K release’s Type to Combo-pack and a bundled Blu-ray card is created and linked to it (two for Combo-pack (dual BD)). The bundled discs share the 4K card’s purchase details, which is why those rows are dimmed on them, and they cannot be deleted on their own. Changing the Type back asks before removing bundled discs you have edited.

Upcoming releases

A release can be cataloged before it comes out: give it a release date up to a year ahead. Until that day it is upcoming. Its card shows Upcoming beside the Purchased checkbox, and its market availability reads Unreleased. Any availability it had recorded is cleared, so the Lowest Price row starts fresh once the disc is out. Upcoming releases are collected in the Upcoming Releases smart list (Chapter 7). Film Tracker can also notify you on each one’s release day (Settings > General, Chapter 11). Nothing needs refreshing: everything about an upcoming release changes on its own on the day it comes out.

The wishlist

Turn on Wishlisted for a release you want. Wishlisted releases are collected, in an order you choose, on the Release Wishlist report (Chapter 8). A release cannot be both purchased and wishlisted, so marking it purchased takes it off the wishlist.

Tip

Searches can ask about releases too: #release dolby vision, #release region free, #release criterion. See Chapter 7 of the Search Guide.

Chapter 7

Lists and smart lists

Your own lists hold whatever you put in them. Smart lists hold whatever matches their rule, and update themselves as your library changes.

Your own lists

  • Create one with File > Create New List… (⌘N), or make one from the films you have selected with Create New List from Selection… (⇧⌘N).
  • Add films by dragging them onto the list in the sidebar, with Film > Add to List, or in the film sheet’s List Membership field.
  • Reorder lists by dragging them in the sidebar.
  • Manage a list by secondary-clicking it: Edit List Name…, Duplicate List (⌥⌘N), Clear List Contents, Delete All List Contents from Library, and Remove List.
Know the difference

Clear List Contents empties the list and leaves the films in your library. Delete All List Contents from Library deletes every film in the list from Film Tracker. Remove List deletes the list itself and leaves its films alone.

A list cannot share a name with a smart list or with a Queue section.

Smart lists

Smart lists appear in the sidebar in six families. Expand a family with its disclosure arrow, or pick a member from the picker above the gallery. Upcoming Releases has no other members, so it has no arrow or picker.

  Unpurchased 4K / Blu-ray / DVDhave a release in that format and have purchased none in it.

Smart lists can be hidden from the sidebar in Settings > General. While they are hidden, they are also unavailable to the List search token.

Special sorting

Special Sorting tags, set in the film sheet’s Details tab, mark films that should be set apart from your regular viewing. A tagged film is excluded from the rotation lists and from the Filtered Library, and appears instead in its tag’s list.

  • Waiting for edit and Temporarily on hold can be combined with each other and with one of the three below.
  • Indefinitely on hold, Ignore, and Blacklisted exclude one another; choosing one clears the others.

Finding a film’s lists

Film > Show in List names every list a film is in and opens the one you choose with the film selected. The contextual sidebar’s List Membership table does the same.

Chapter 8

Recents and Reports

Recents shows what has changed lately. Reports turns the whole library into tables, rankings, and statistics.

Recents

Each tab is ordered newest first and shows up to 200 films.

TabShows

Recently AddedFilms by the date they entered the library.

Reports

The Reports view (⌘4) has five tabs. The share button in its title bar opens the PDF export (Chapter 10).

Purchase and Expense Tracker

Every purchased release with its cost, the amount spent, the purchase date, studio, condition, and notes. Click a column heading to sort by it.

Price Tracker

Every unpurchased release with a lowest price recorded, and when that price was seen. Use it to spot a deal against what you have seen before.

Hall of Fame

Your personal ranking of up to 200 films. Add a film with the Hall of Fame checkbox in the film sheet; it joins at the bottom. Drag rows to reorder them. The report also shows the best format you own each film in, and when a better format is available.

Release Wishlist

Every wishlisted release, with its format, studio, release date (or year, when that is all that is recorded), packaging, and market availability. Drag rows to set the order of priority.

Visualizations and Insights

A long, scrolling page of charts and figures drawn from everything you have entered. The sections include:

  • Library Overview and Ownership
  • Needs Attention: overdue rotations, missing posters, IDs, or artwork, unrated films, and more
  • Collection Growth and Ratings Breakdown
  • Viewing History: viewings per year, rewatches, seasonality, versions watched
  • Rotation: what is overdue or due soon, and whether your thresholds match your habits
  • Purchases and Spending, Spending Trends, and Value Realized (cost per viewing)
  • Wishlist and Duplicates and Upgrades
  • Studios and Labels and Regions
  • Release Decades, Runtime, and Picture
  • People and Genres, and Audio, Sources, and Technical

Some sections have their own controls, such as a time span, that apply to that section only. Charts fill in as you record viewing dates, purchase dates, and prices; a section with too little data says so rather than showing an empty chart.

Chapter 9

Importing

Two imports fill the library for you: film information from tagged video files, and your ratings from Blu-ray.com. Both are in File and in the Imports view.

Films from MP4/M4V files

Choose File > Import Metadata… (⇧⌘I) and select video files, a folder, or both. Film Tracker reads the iTunes-style metadata in each file. The video itself is never copied or changed.

From the file’s tagsFills in
Title, release date, genreName, Release Date, Genre
Content ratingMPAA rating
Movie informationCast, directors, producers, screenwriters, studio
Composer, executive producerComposer, Executive Producer
Description, commentsSynopsis, iTunes Comments
Sort name, encoding toolSort Name, Encoding Tools
ArtworkPoster
The video itselfDuration, aspect ratio, and digital source (inferred from the comments or the resolution)

A file whose title and year match a film already in the library is a duplicate. By default duplicates are skipped; with Update existing information for duplicates on (Settings > Import), the existing film takes the file’s values wherever they differ.

Ratings from Blu-ray.com

Save your ratings page from Blu-ray.com as an HTML file, then choose File > Import Ratings… (⌥⌘I) and select it. Each rating is matched to a film by title and year, then by alternate titles for any left over. Ratings that match no film are listed as Not Found and discarded. A film that already has a rating keeps it, unless Update existing ratings is on.

The Imports view

The Imports view has a Metadata tab and a Ratings tab. Each shows the import in progress, with a Cancel button, and a card for every past import that counts the items added, updated, skipped as duplicates, not found, and failed. Click a count to see the titles involved. Clear This Item and Clear All tidy the history; they do not undo the imports.

Matching settings

In Settings > Import:

  • Fuzzy title matching tolerates small differences in titles when detecting duplicates and pairing ratings, which finds many more matches.
  • Fuzzy year matching for ratings accepts a year one off in either direction when pairing ratings.
  • Scan subfolders for items includes files inside subfolders of a folder you select.
  • Always override the “Sort Name” field replaces a file’s own sort name with one Film Tracker generates consistently.
Chapter 10

Keeping the library healthy

Find and merge duplicates, keep backups you can restore from, and print the library to PDF.

Scanning for duplicates

File > Scan Library for Duplicates… (⇧⌘D), then Start Scan. Each pair found is shown side by side with its differences (year, rating, genre, studio, date added).

  • Reconcile: click the poster of the film to keep, then Reconcile. The other film is deleted.
  • Ignore: the pair is not a duplicate. Ignored pairs are left out of later scans until you reset them in Settings > Advanced.

Backups

A backup is a single .ftbackup file that holds your entire library: films, lists, releases, and viewing history, plus your settings and poster images if you include them.

KindHow
AutomaticTurn on Automatic database backup in Settings > General. A backup is made in the background shortly after each launch, and only the three most recent are kept. Reveal Backups in Finder shows where they are.
ManualFile > Export Database… (⇧⌘E). Choose a destination, choose what to include (the library is always included; User Settings and Poster Images are optional), then Export Backup.

Restoring

  1. File > Restore Database… (⇧⌘R) and Select Backup File….
  2. Check the summary: when it was made, by which version of the app, and how many films, lists, releases, and posters it holds.
  3. Click Restore Database and confirm. When it finishes, click Quit and Restart.
Restoring replaces everything

A restore permanently replaces the whole library with the backup’s contents; nothing is merged. If you might want your current library back, export a backup of it first.

Exporting to PDF

In the same Export tab (or from the share button in Reports), choose which sections to include, then Export PDF: Complete Library, Viewing History, Disc-based Releases, Hall of Fame, and Visualizations and Insights. The file is named FilmTracker Library with the date and time.

Complete Data flags

Turn on Show “Complete Data” components in Settings > Advanced to mark films whose information you have finished, then filter by Complete Data or Incomplete Data to see what is left to do.

Settings

Film Tracker > Settings… (⌘,). Switch tabs with ⌥1 through ⌥5.

General

SettingEffect
System calendar integrationLets Add Viewing Event also create an event in your calendar.
Default viewing rotation thresholdThe rotation period for every film without an override. Reset All Overrides clears every override.
Show a warning before…Confirmations before deleting films, deleting releases, changing lists (choose lists and their contents, lists only, or contents only), and queuing films that carry Special Sorting tags.
Notify me about…A notification on the release day of each release cataloged while its date was still ahead, at 9:00 AM, whether or not Film Tracker is open. Choose all new releases or wishlisted ones only. Turning it on asks macOS for permission to notify.
Large poster imagesAt most six grid columns instead of eight, for bigger posters on large windows.
Show the status barThe film count along the bottom of the window.
Show the Smart Lists sectionShows or hides smart lists. Beneath it: keep queued films out of the Up for Rotation lists.
When dragging films onto the Queue…Makes the Queue a drop target, and picks the section dropped films join.
Contextual sidebarShow the Web Query button; keep the same tab when the selection changes; expand collapsed tables automatically; show additional Viewing History details; show a summary table of all releases.
Persist selection when exiting search resultsKeeps a film selected when you clear a search, if it is also in the view you return to.
Remember prior scroll positionsEach view reopens where you left it.
Remember the last-selected tabRecents, Reports, or both reopen on the tab you last used, across launches.
Remember the last-selected view optionsEach view keeps its own sort, filter, and grid or list style across launches.
Automatic database backupA backup after each launch, keeping three. Reveal Backups in Finder; Delete Existing Backups.

Import

Scan subfolders for items; Fuzzy title matching; Fuzzy year matching for ratings; Always override the “Sort Name” field; Update existing information for duplicates; Update existing ratings. See Chapter 9.

Export and Restore

Backups and PDF export, and restoring from a backup. See Chapter 10.

Advanced

SettingEffect
Show “Complete Data” componentsAdds the Complete Data flag and filters. Reset All Flags clears every flag.
Ignored duplicate pairsReset All Ignored Pairs lets the next duplicate scan find them again.
Show glyphs in contextual sidebar fieldsSmall icons beside the fields in the Details tab.
Override date clamp for viewing eventsAllows viewing dates earlier than a film’s release date. Turns itself off when you quit.
Strict input validation for HandBrake frame dataChecks frame numbers and time codes in the Notes tab more strictly as you enter them.
Prune orphaned poster imagesPeriodically deletes poster files that no film uses any more.
Debug loggingWrites a diagnostic log, useful when reporting a bug.

Searching

Type #, a field, a space, and a value, then press Return. Prefix with / for OR and ! for NOT. The Film Tracker Search Guide covers every token.
